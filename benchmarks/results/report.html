Every backend oxvox ships, against scipy's cKDTree and Open3D's hybrid search, over five synthetic pointcloud families and two real laser scans. Build time and query time are measured separately (warm-up, then the median of three), each index is built and queried in its own worker process, and every exact implementation's answers are checked against scipy's.

How much the approximate backend misses

Recall of the graph backend against scipy's answers, on the 2048-query correctness subsample: the fraction of the true neighbours it returned. It never returns a point outside the radius, so what recall measures is omission, not error.

Over 276 checks its kNN recall has a median of 1.0000, a minimum of 0.9853, and is at least 0.99 on 98% of them. Counting is the workload it cannot do: its exact-count agreement falls as low as 2.3% of query points on the dense clouds, because a flood that stops at the k-th best neighbour has no reason to visit every point in the radius.

Correctness

Every exact implementation has to reproduce scipy's distances. Index sets are only compared where the distances are all distinct, since a tie lets any implementation return either point.

The exact backends reproduce scipy's neighbour counts on every check but 25 of 552, and there they differ by one neighbour in 0.2% of query points, only on real scan A, real scan B, scene at 0.05 m, 0.2 m, 0.422419 m. That is the radius boundary, not a bug: scipy counts a point at exactly the radius, oxvox does not (d² < r²), and a real scan's quantised coordinates put points exactly there.
